01

Why I built it

I studied Chinese at a language centre myself. As I learnt (starting from HSK 0), I noticed a few problems that made it hard for me and my classmates to make progress. Most people struggle with writing Chinese characters, so I built an app that makes learners write a lot. Reading hanzi without pinyin is hard too, so the app also makes learners read more. And learning vocabulary without seeing it used in real situations is boring, so the app uses AI to put words into real situations and contexts.

At first it was hard to work out when my app could be called "done". People expect a lot from a Chinese app, or any language learning app. We already have Duolingo and Falou, and in Vietnam there is the Hanzii dictionary app. I realised that matching all their features wasn't realistic. So I defined the core features that solve exactly the pain points above, and focused only on building and fine-tuning those, so I could ship and get the app on the store as soon as possible.

Tech stack

From the start, I wasn't aiming to build an app that could handle heavy load. I wanted to ship fast while keeping the scalability and security it needed, so wherever a third-party service did the job well (sign-in, hosting, payments), I used it rather than building from scratch.

03

Building it

These are the three main challenges I faced while building the app. There were plenty of others, of course, but these took the most time and effort.

Challenge

User experience

An app that impresses people, earns their trust and gets used a lot needs a smooth, effective user experience. So I spent most of my time building, refining and optimising the app's UX, from the UI all the way to the backend. And rather than relying on my own instincts, I asked friends to be testers and adjusted the flow wherever they got stuck or confused.

Challenge

Cleaning up the dictionary

The open-source dictionary I started from was a mess, and a Chinese learning app revolves around its dictionary. I spent a fair amount of tokens building an AI workflow to classify words, classify meanings, fix definitions and more, across nearly 130,000 words.

Challenge

Testing

Testing features and hunting down bugs turned out to take a lot of time and effort, especially for payments. It looks simple, but it hides a lot of edge cases that have to be handled before going on the store.

Marketing

I posted videos introducing the app on TikTok, but they didn't bring in many downloads. Most of my users were classmates from my language centre and people I know who also learn Chinese.

06

Advice for app builders

If you're building an app, try to finish it quickly. Quick doesn't mean sloppy, or shipping something that feels like AI slop. It means getting the UI/UX right and covering the security and core backend logic. Once the app was shipped, I kept building on users' feedback, so I didn't waste time on features nobody needs while neglecting the ones that matter.
